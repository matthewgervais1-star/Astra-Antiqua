# The culture marks

Five cultures, and more than one mark each. This document holds the decision,
the research behind it, and the collisions I had to resolve across the set.

## Three tiers, because risk lives in placement

A mark's meaning is not fixed by the mark. It is fixed by where the mark sits
and what stands next to it. A launcher icon is alone on a home screen with
nothing to explain it, so it gets read at its most loaded. The same mark inside
a captioned page about Roman magistracy is just accurate. That difference, not
a symbol's reputation, is what decides where each one belongs.

| Tier | Where it appears | What it optimises for |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The launcher icon** | App icon, one per culture, six with neutral | Distinctness from its four siblings at 48px, context free, no colour reliance |
| **2. In-app emblems** | Culture doorways, section heads, share cards, chapter marks | Recognition. Always beside a label, so the famous mark is the right mark |
| **3. Editorial illustration** | Method page, voices pages, anywhere the app explains rather than brands | Accuracy. The full historical repertoire, captions doing the work captions do |

## Tier 1: the launcher icons

Researched by five parallel briefs, one per culture, each required to find marks
attested in the material record, meaning what that culture's surface in this app
actually does, and holding at 48px.

| Culture | Mark | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| **Egypt** | The balance of the weighing, in its Papyrus of Ani form: feather finial, crossbar and two pans, baboon of Thoth, plumb bob steadied at the base | The device that does the weighing, not a metaphor for it. The most repeated judgment image in the Egyptian corpus across roughly 1500 years |
| **Greece** | The Delphic tripod: three converging legs under a shallow bowl | The recurring image on Delphic coinage for centuries. The furniture where a petitioner's question met the god's answer |
| **Canaan** | Ugaritic alphabetic cuneiform: a cluster of driven wedge signs | The world's earliest alphabet, Ugarit's own invention, and the medium the vow and the docket were written in |
| **Carthage** | The cothon: the circular naval harbour at Salammbo in plan, a broken ring of ship sheds around an offset island | Appian's description matches the excavated remains. Civil infrastructure for auditing ships in and out |
| **Rome** | The lituus: the augur's curved staff with the shaft knop from Caesar's denarii | The instrument an augur held to mark the templum before declaring a day lawful |

Each is an instrument or a procedure rather than a deity portrait, which keeps
the set legible at icon size and keeps the app out of rendering gods.

### The collisions, and how each resolves

Ten pairs. Three were caught independently by researchers who could not see each
other's work, which is the argument for having briefed them separately.

| Pair | Risk | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Egypt / Rome | Roman Iustitia also carries a scale | Resolved by choice. Rome takes a staff, so Egypt keeps the balance |
| Greece / Carthage | The sign of Tanit is a triangle under a disc, and so is a tripod at 48px | Resolved by choice. Carthage takes the harbour, so Greece keeps the tripod |
| Canaan / Carthage | The closest related cultures in the set. Carthage is a Phoenician colony sharing Baal and Astarte | Resolved structurally. Wedge cuneiform and a harbour plan share no stroke primitive. The hardest pair became the most separable |
| Canaan / Carthage, script | Punic script IS Phoenician script at a later stage, invisibly different at 48px | Carthage's script candidate ruled out for this reason. Only Canaan uses letterforms |
| Greece / Carthage, second order | A tripod with radiating legs reads as a triskelion, and the cothon is radial ticks around a hub. Both become spokes from a centre | Enforced in the drawing. The tripod's legs converge under a bowl as load bearing furniture; the cothon's ring encloses and breaks only at the channel mouth |
| Egypt / Greece | Both are a vertical with mass at the top | Acceptable. Egypt's weight is a wide horizontal beam with hanging pans, the tripod's is a compact bowl |
| Carthage / Rome | A spiral head and a ring are both closed curves | Acceptable. The lituus is a staff with a small terminal spiral, the cothon is a ring filling the field |
| The remaining four pairs | No shared grammar | Clean |

### Rendering constraints

Each is a way the mark fails if drawn carelessly, not a stylistic preference.

- **Egypt.** A bare symmetric beam collapses into the scales of justice glyph.
  Keep the feather finial, the baboon, and the visibly off axis plumb bob.
- **Greece.** Three distinct converging legs, never a solid triangular mass, or
  it drifts toward Tanit's grammar. Shallow off axis bowl. Drop the ring handles.
- **Canaan.** Wedges must read as driven strokes with corner and linear forms,
  not tally marks. A cold viewer reads "Mesopotamia" before "Ugarit"; the set's
  context carries it and a label closes the gap.
- **Carthage.** The ring must not read as a gear, sun or wheel. Asymmetric
  channel break, off centre island, short ship shed ticks inside the ring rather
  than spokes crossing to the middle.
- **Rome.** A tight closed spiral with the shaft knop, not an open shepherd's
  crook. The lituus is the probable ancestor of the bishop's crozier and a loose
  rendering reads ecclesiastical.

## Tier 2: in-app emblems

The marks each culture is known by. They belong where a label stands beside
them, which is most of the app: culture doorways, section heads, share cards.

| Culture | Emblem |
|---|---|
| Egypt | The wedjat, or the ankh where the context is life rather than judgment |
| Greece | Athena's owl, from the Athenian tetradrachm |
| Canaan | No popular emblem exists. Cuneiform carries both tiers here |
| Carthage | The sign of Tanit |
| Rome | The aquila |

Canaan's gap is worth stating rather than papering over: it has no mark a
general audience recognises, which is exactly why an emblem-first icon set would
have broken. In tier 2, beside the word "Canaan" and the council passage,
cuneiform reads fine.

## Tier 3: editorial illustration

The full repertoire, used where the app explains. This includes the fasces, on a
page about magistrates and imperium, captioned. A product that tells readers the
Canaanite divine council was absorbed into monotheism, and that three of its
planets are modern inventions wearing ancient names, has no business flinching
from a bundle of rods.

## A correction

An earlier draft of this research grouped the fasces, the aquila and SPQR
together as politically contaminated and ruled out all three. That was wrong on
two of them. The aquila is on the arms of Germany, Poland, Mexico and the United
States, and Rome flew it for centuries before any of them existed. SPQR is the
current legal abbreviation of the Comune di Roma, cast into the city's drain
covers. Treating either as forfeit because extremists adopted it concedes
territory for nothing.

The fasces is narrowly different, in that Mussolini took his movement's name
from it, and it is on the ADL's hate symbol list. That is a reason to keep it
out of tier 1, where nothing explains it. It is not a reason to pretend Rome
did not have lictors.

What actually decided tier 1 was never contamination. It was meaning. The
aquila is a legionary standard: it means military authority, not reading the
sky. SPQR is a political slogan. The app's Rome surface is THE AUSPICES OF THE
DAY, and the lituus is the instrument that does that job.

## What the research refused, and why

- **Sign of Tanit as Carthage's icon.** Its densest attestation is on the tophet
  stelae at the centre of the contested child sacrifice question; it carries
  live modern reappropriation; and its triangle bar disc grammar is the hardest
  in the set to separate from Canaan, because it IS Carthage's development of
  Canaanite iconography. It moves to tier 2, where a label disarms all three.
- **The massebah for Canaan.** The object the Hebrew Bible commands torn down.
  It would read as taking a position in a live religious argument.
- **The crescent and disc.** At 48px it is indistinguishable from the star and
  crescent, which postdates it by over a millennium and has no connection to it.
- **The Antikythera mechanism.** The tempting wrong answer: conceptually perfect
  for an app that computes an ephemeris, and the wrong register entirely, a
  scientific instrument in a surface built on shrine and petition. A gear reads
  as "settings" in any interface.
- **The ordinary cliches.** The ankh and the Eye of Horus as icons, pyramids,
  columns, laurel, the Colosseum, the Capitoline Wolf.

## Residual risks

- **Cold recognisability is uneven.** Egypt's balance and Rome's lituus read
  immediately. Canaan's wedges and Carthage's cothon need the product to have
  taught "the docket" and "the harbour" first. Onboarding and the method page
  already do this, and the icons lean on it.
- **The set mixes categories:** three instruments, one script, one architectural
  plan. Defensible, since all five are what that culture used to read or record
  fate, but it is a choice worth making deliberately.
