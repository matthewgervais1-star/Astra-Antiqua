# Prompt: the neutral icon, the logotype, and the brand system

Paste into Claude Design. It produces a canvas of artboards, not code.

## What this one covers, and what it does not

This is one of six prompts that replace a single combining brief. The five culture launcher
marks each get their own prompt. This prompt covers everything in the identity that is not
per-culture: the neutral icon a reader sees before choosing a culture, the drawn ASTRA
ANTIQVA wordmark, the brand system sheet, and the open question about Cinzel.

No culture launcher mark and no in-app emblem is drawn as art on this canvas. The five
culture marks appear once, as 48px thumbnail sketches on the single board named
proof-family-check, so the neutral icon can be checked for family membership. Nothing on
that board is an asset.

## Why the boards are named and separated

The earlier brief asked for the whole icon set on shared comparison boards, so the marks
came back combined and nothing exported as a single asset. The export contract inside the
fence fixes that: one deliverable per artboard, systematic names, and composite boards
allowed only under a `proof-` name. The Android adaptive icon is two layers, so its
foreground and background are separate boards and must never be flattened.

## The one place exploration is still open

Every other mark in this commission is decided by research. The neutral icon is not, which
is why section 1 asks for two or three directions and an argument for the one to ship.

## What comes back

Twenty-three boards. Ten export as assets. Three are direction studies for the neutral
icon. Two are reference documents. Eight are labelled proof sheets and ship nothing.

```text
You are designing the parts of the brand identity for Astra Antiqua that are not tied to a
single culture: the neutral app icon, the drawn wordmark, and the brand system sheet.

THE SCOPE OF THIS COMMISSION, WHICH IS A HARD BOUNDARY
Five culture launcher marks and five in-app culture emblems exist in this product. They are
decided by research and drawn under separate briefs. You are not drawing any of them. No
culture launcher mark and no culture emblem appears as art on any board you produce, with
one exception: the board named proof-family-check carries the five launcher marks as 48px
thumbnail sketches so the neutral icon can be checked against the family it joins. Those
thumbnails are sketch fidelity, they are never exported, and nothing on that board stands as
final art for any culture.

THE PRODUCT, IN ONE PARAGRAPH
One sky is computed from a real ephemeris, and five ancient cultures read that same sky
through their own theology. Egypt weighs it in the Hall of Ma'at. Greece questions it at
the day's oracle. Canaan convenes the council of El. Carthage audits it in the decree of
the Tophet. Rome takes the auspices. The same planetary placement keeps the same core
meaning in every voice, and each culture's reading is native to its institutions: the scale
and the feather, the tripod and the petition, the vow and the docket, the ledger and the
harbour, the censor's list. The product's claim is honesty rather than mysticism. It states
plainly that positions are computed and nothing is invented, and it says so on a method
page. Three modern bodies, Uranus, Neptune and Pluto, are carried as declared receptions,
admitted under native names because no ancient culture knew them.

THE IDENTITY IT ALREADY HAS
The interface is called "The Instrument in the Temple". It is austere by decision: flat
culture-tinted grounds, one muted accent per culture, square corners everywhere, no
gradients, no shadows, no glass, no glow. The only ornament is an incised 1px rule. Type is
Cinzel for display in caps with wide tracking, Spectral for prose, IBM Plex Mono for data
and microcopy. It should read like a well-set almanac or a museum wall label.

Grounds and accents, exact:
  Egypt     ground #1A1712   accent #B59A55  (gold)
  Greece    ground #171A1C   accent #7C93A6  (aegean)
  Canaan    ground #1A1813   accent #8A9268  (cedar)
  Carthage  ground #191218   accent #94648C  (tyrian)
  Rome      ground #1B1410   accent #A5544A  (pompeian red)
  Neutral   ground #171310   accent #B59A55
Inks: bone #E6DECC, prose #D9D1BF, dim #9A917D, faint #6F6B60.
Signals: harmony #B59A55, conflict #B56B71 (also retrograde and destructive actions).
Rules: rule rgb(230 222 204 / 0.16), row rule rgb(230 222 204 / 0.11). 1px only.
Use these values verbatim. Do not sample, tint, or approximate them.

THE EXPORT CONTRACT, WHICH IS NOT NEGOTIABLE

Every deliverable asset gets its own artboard. One mark per board.

  1. Board names are exactly as listed in the manifest at the end. Print each board's name
     as a label outside the art so an export is traceable to a spec line.
  2. Board dimensions are the export dimensions. Draw the mark at the size the board says.
     Do not park a small drawing inside an oversized board.
  3. A composite board is allowed only when its name begins with "proof-". A proof sheet is
     a proof. Nothing is ever exported from one, and no proof sheet substitutes for an
     asset board.
  4. The Android adaptive icon is two layers. The board icon-neutral-fg holds the
     foreground alone on a transparent board. The board icon-neutral-bg holds a flat colour
     fill and nothing else. Never flatten the pair into one image, and never paint the
     ground into the foreground board.
  5. Transparent means transparent. A near-black rectangle standing in for transparency
     ships as a black square behind a mask.
  6. No asset board carries two versions of the same asset side by side, and no asset board
     carries a comparison strip, a size ladder, or a family row. Those belong on a proof
     sheet.
  7. No asset board carries annotation inside the live area. Every note, dimension, caption
     and argument sits outside the art, or on a proof sheet, or on a reference board.
  8. Two board categories are documents rather than marks: system-sheet and type-question.
     The one-mark rule does not bind them. They still carry no icon asset and no logotype
     asset, and nothing is exported from them.

1. THE NEUTRAL ICON

The icon a reader sees before choosing a culture, on ground #171310 with accent #B59A55.
It appears at onboarding, sign-in, account, wait-list and error, and it is the app icon
until a culture is picked.

WHAT IT HAS TO BELONG TO. Five culture marks ship beside it. You are not drawing them. You
need their grammar so the neutral reads as a sixth member of one set:

  Egypt     a balance: a wide horizontal beam, two hanging pans, a feather finial, the
            baboon of Thoth, an off-axis plumb bob at the base
  Greece    the Delphic tripod: three distinct converging legs under a shallow bowl
  Canaan    Ugaritic alphabetic cuneiform: a cluster of driven wedge signs
  Carthage  the cothon harbour in plan: a broken ring of ship sheds around an offset island
  Rome      the lituus: a long thin uniformly curved shaft with a tight terminal spiral and
            a shaft knop

FAMILY RULES, ABSOLUTE. These are stated as rules rather than as comparisons because you
cannot see the five finished marks from here and cannot check the family for yourself. The
neutral obeys all four:

  - It is an instrument, a procedure, or a piece of the product's own construction, never a
    deity portrait and never a figure. That is what keeps the set legible at icon size and
    keeps the app out of rendering gods.
  - It is a line construction at one stroke weight. Nothing fills into a solid mass.
  - Asymmetry is load-bearing wherever symmetry would read as machinery or as a stock
    glyph. Every regularity you add moves the mark toward a system icon.
  - It survives at 48px and in a single ink. Accent hue is never the only thing separating
    it from another mark in the set.

HOW THE NEUTRAL ICON DIES. Four ways, and the fourth is the one that kills it. Each carries
the construction that defends against it.

  Fails as a sixth culture. If it reads as another pantheon, the set stops meaning "before
  you have chosen" and starts meaning "there are six of them".
  Defence: the mark comes from the product's own construction rather than from any
  historical repertoire. No attribute, no creature, no crown, no script, no plan of a
  building. If a caption could name a civilisation for it, redraw it.

  Fails as generic mysticism. No zodiac wheel, no starfield, no orb, no crystal ball, no
  nebula. This product's whole differentiator is that it is not that.
  Defence: nothing radiates, nothing twinkles, nothing floats. Straight cuts and right
  angles carry the mark, and the field edge is drawn rather than implied.

  Fails by being too quiet. The identity is muted, and muted is not absent. This mark holds
  a square on a home screen beside saturated commercial icons.
  Defence: accent #B59A55 on ground #171310 measures about 6.8:1. Use the whole of it. The
  mark occupies 60 to 70 percent of the canvas width and the stroke never falls below 3px
  at 48. A mark that reads as an empty tile at arm's length has failed.

  Fails as a system icon. This is the real risk, because the neutral mark is abstract while
  the other five are objects. A doorway reads as "exit". A gear reads as "settings". A
  horizontal rule reads as "menu" or "divider". A V reads as "confirm". A downward chevron
  reads as "download". A square with a corner turned reads as "file".
  Defence: the mark carries one deliberate irregularity that no interface glyph has, and
  that irregularity has to survive at 48px. System icons are symmetric, centred, and
  reduced to the fewest strokes that name a function. Break one of those three on purpose
  and say in a note which one you broke. Before you test the mark against antiquity, hold
  it beside the icon vocabulary of an operating system and see what it gets mistaken for.

THE SHAPES ALREADY SPENT, WHICH THE NEUTRAL MAY NOT TAKE. This list is absolute. You cannot
see the fifty small deity glyphs that live elsewhere in this product, so you cannot check
these for yourself. The five launcher marks above plus those fifty glyphs have used most of
the obvious territory. Everything here is claimed:

  - a plain disc, bare or rayed, and a disc with a uraeus, and a disc held in a cup
  - a crescent, closed or open, alone or cradling a disc
  - a shallow arc with dots on it, and a wide shallow arc with hooks at its base
  - a vertical shaft with something at the top: sceptre, caduceus, trident, thunderbolt,
    spear, key, hooked blade
  - a small ring on a stem, and a small ring with a right-angle tooth
  - a crossing-line star of four to eight points
  - two curves meeting at a vertex: horns, jaws
  - a serpent S-bend, and an open coil that never closes
  - two tall parallel plumes, and two stacked horizontal bars
  - a single pure uniform curve with a plain ball terminal, reserved for the lituus alone
    anywhere in this product

One rectilinear shape is also taken: a low profile seat with a back, the Egyptian throne
sign. That is the only right-angled construction in the whole fifty, which means the
rectilinear field is where the neutral has room. Right angles, an enclosed field, a plain
straight cut, and the incised rule itself are open.

THREE DIRECTIONS TO START FROM. Explore two or three. These, or better ones you find. Each
direction goes on its own board.

  A. THE THRESHOLD. A rectilinear aperture: two jambs and a lintel, open at the foot, drawn
     as line at one weight. The temple entered before any culture is chosen. It comes from
     the product rather than from a repertoire, and the app's own header component is
     already called the lintel. Its risk is the system-icon failure above: an aperture is
     one small step from an exit sign or an inbox tray. Whatever separates it has to be
     visible at 48px, and proportion alone will not do it.

  B. THE INCISED RULE. The only ornament the identity permits, promoted to the mark: a
     single horizontal rule crossing a ruled field, cut once by a short vertical. The mark
     is the product's grammar rather than an object, which no other icon in the set can
     claim. Its risk is silence. A rule at 48px can read as a minus sign or as nothing, so
     the field edge has to be part of the construction rather than a border around it.

  C. THE CLASSICAL V. The V from ANTIQVA cut as an inscribed letterform, tying the icon to
     the wordmark. Its risk is double: a bare V reads as a confirm tick, and one culture in
     this set is drawn as a cluster of angular driven strokes, so a bare V drifts toward
     that family. If you take this direction, the V has to read as inscription: a cut
     trough with real width, a flat terminal at each arm, held inside a ruled field.

Keep every direction on the canvas, one per board. Say which you would ship and argue it on
proof-neutral-failure against three tests: it reads as "no culture yet" rather than as a
sixth culture, it holds at 48px in one ink, and it clears the spent-shape list above. Where
you think a direction I named is wrong, say so in a note and show the better one on its own
direction board rather than quietly substituting it.

PLATFORM MECHANICS FOR THE NEUTRAL ICON

Draw the shipped mark at 512x512 on ground #171310 with the mark in #B59A55.

Android adaptive icon: the canvas is 108x108dp, authored here at 432x432px. Every element
that carries meaning sits inside the centred 288px circle, which is the middle 72dp. The
outer ring is masked away to a circle, a squircle or a rounded square depending on the
launcher. The foreground goes on its own transparent board. The background is a flat
#171310 fill and nothing else. Keep the mark's extremities inside the safe zone with room
to spare, since some launchers crop harder than the spec.

iOS takes a single opaque 1024x1024 square with no transparency and no layers. Do not round
its corners or apply a mask. The system does both.

At 48x48 draw the mark at true size rather than scaling the 512 down. If a stroke needs to
thicken or an element needs to drop to survive there, do it, and record what changed in the
note on proof-neutral-scale rather than on the 48 board itself.

Monochrome: one bone ink #E6DECC on transparent, for notification icons and favicons. If
the mark depends on the accent to hold together, it has failed and needs redrawing by form.

2. THE LOGOTYPE

The product is written ASTRA ANTIQVA, with the classical V standing for U. That stays and
is not a stylistic tic to be softened.

Draw it as a wordmark. The current wordmark is Cinzel set at 0.3em tracking, which is why
it reads competent and anonymous. Cinzel is itself a Trajan derivative, so a drawn wordmark
that comes back as a slightly different Trajan has cost work and bought nothing. Name the
difference in one sentence on proof-logotype-alphabet, then make that sentence visible in
the letters.

Where the difference can live, given this identity forbids gradients, shadows and bevels:

  - Proportion. The Roman inscriptional width system runs wide on O Q C D G and narrow on
    E F L S. This word has no O, so the width contrast has to show in S against T and in Q
    against I, or the system is invisible and you have drawn a monowidth face by accident.
  - Stroke contrast. Thick and thin, set by the angle of a flat tool.
  - Terminals. A serif that reads as the end of a chisel cut, not as a bracketed book serif.
  - Junctions. The A apex, the V vertex, the R leg, the Q tail.
  - Counters. What the A and the R enclose.

One signature move, not eight. Q's tail and R's leg have the most room. Pick one to carry
the identity and keep every other letter quiet.

Two specific problems to solve rather than avoid:
  - The word seam. ASTRA ends in A and ANTIQVA begins with A. Two facing diagonals across a
    word space open a hole that no even tracking value closes. Fix it in the spacing and
    show the fix on proof-logotype-alphabet.
  - Tracking. The drawn forms should not need 0.3em to look intentional. Set the tracking
    the letters want and state the value.

Sitting beside Cinzel. The wordmark meets Cinzel on the same rule in two places: the header
at 17px with 0.3em tracking, and the tool bar at 14px with 0.28em tracking, with Cinzel caps
navigation at 12px and 0.22em tracking beside it. Cap height, stroke weight and apparent
colour have to match there, or the header reads as two families. That comparison is a
composite, so it goes on proof-logotype-beside-cinzel and never on an asset board.

The monogram. Options are the two A's, an A with a V, or the V alone. Draw the options on
proof-logotype-monogram and the chosen one alone on logotype-monogram. If you also propose
the V as a neutral-icon direction, the two commissions collide on a home screen and in a
favicon. Decide which one owns the V, say so in a note, and show the loser's replacement.

On light. The identity is dark only, so the light version is for print, press and a light
favicon: ground #E6DECC, ink #171310. The board logotype-on-light carries the horizontal
lockup alone. Letterforms drawn light-on-dark look heavier than they measure, so state
whether the inverted version needs a weight adjustment. If it does, the before and after
pair goes on proof-logotype-beside-cinzel, since two versions of one asset may not share an
asset board.

Sizes and clear space. State the smallest pixel height at which the horizontal lockup
holds, where the stacked version takes over, where the monogram takes over, and the clear
space around each, expressed in cap heights. All of that goes on proof-logotype-scale. The
three lockup boards stay clean.

3. THE BRAND SYSTEM SHEET

One artboard, system-sheet, on ground #171310. It is a reference document rather than an
asset: no icon and no logotype is drawn on it. Four blocks and a strip.

PALETTE, with role names beside every swatch. Grounds are page backgrounds, one per
culture, with the neutral ground used only where no culture is presumed. Accents are one
muted metal per culture. Inks are bone for headings and primary text, prose for body, dim
for secondary and microcopy, faint for the quietest labels. Signals are harmony and
conflict. Print the contrast ratio against the neutral ground beside each ink: bone about
14:1, dim about 6:1, faint about 3.5:1, accent gold about 6.8:1. Mark faint ink explicitly
as legal for rules and large type and illegal for microcopy, because microcopy floors at
11px in this product and 3.5:1 does not carry it.

TYPE SCALE, as the product uses it:
  Header wordmark       Cinzel 500, 17px, tracking 0.3em
  Tool bar wordmark     Cinzel 500, 14px, tracking 0.28em
  Navigation caps       Cinzel, 12px, tracking 0.22em
  Headline              Cinzel, 40 to 58px, line-height 1.25, tracking 0.1em
  Section divider       Cinzel, 11px, tracking 0.24em, centred between two rules
  Votive instruction    Cinzel, 13 to 15px, tracking 0.14em, in the culture accent
  Prose                 Spectral 300 to 400 with italic, 15 to 18px, line-height 1.65 to 1.75
  Kicker                IBM Plex Mono, 11px, tracking 0.2em, dim
  Data rows             IBM Plex Mono, 11.5px
  Microcopy floor       11px, no exceptions

RULE WEIGHTS. Every rule in this product is 1px at every viewport. There is no 2px rule and
no stacked hairline. Show three: the structural rule at rgb(230 222 204 / 0.16) for headers,
footers, section dividers and column separators; the row rule at rgb(230 222 204 / 0.11)
for table rows; and the accent rule, 64px wide and 1px tall, that sits under a kicker. A
kicker is the short mono label that stands above a block of prose. Show the accent-tinted
border too, the accent at 48 percent alpha, used on chips and bordered boxes. Show the
focus outline: 1px accent, 2px offset, square.

WHAT THE ACCENT IS ALLOWED TO DO. Three jobs and no fourth.
  1. It marks the active culture. The ground tint pairs with it, the culture chip carries
     it, and the active cell of the horizon control fills with it and takes ground-coloured
     text. The culture chip is the small bordered label naming the chosen culture in the
     tool bar. The horizon control is a four-cell segmented row, D W S Y, one bordered row
     of cells with exactly one active.
  2. It carries the votive instruction, bordered above and below, centred, two lines at most.
  3. It marks the received bodies. Uranus, Neptune and Pluto take accent-coloured rows and
     glyphs in the chart table, and a gold rule separates those three from the ancient seven
     wherever the bodies are listed as a set.
The accent is never decorative. It does not wash a background, tint a panel, fill a shape
for emphasis, or make something feel special.

DO AND DO NOT, a strip across the bottom. Draw each side rather than listing words.
  DO: 1px rules. Square corners. Flat ground. One accent per surface. Empty space doing the
  work. Type carrying the ornament's job.
  DO NOT: gradients. Drop shadows. Bevels. Glow. Rounded corners. Two rule weights on one
  surface. A second accent on one surface. Decorative rules that separate nothing.
  Ornamental dingbats. Texture, paper grain, aged parchment. Starfields.

4. THE TYPE QUESTION, optional and answered honestly

One artboard, type-question. It is a reference document rather than an asset: no icon and
no logotype asset is drawn on it.

Cinzel is the default choice for anything Roman, which makes it both apt and generic. Say
whether you would keep it. If not, propose exactly one alternative display face with a
reason grounded in this product.

What the face has to survive here, which is narrower than taste:
  - Caps only, at tracking from 0.06em to 0.3em, at sizes from 11px to 58px. The hard case
    is 11px caps at 0.24em tracking, where most display faces close up or go ragged.
  - A V that carries ANTIQVA without looking like a typo.
  - A drawn wordmark sitting beside it on the same rule, so it has to be a face the wordmark
    can be a sibling of.
  - One script fetches all three families from Google Fonts for both the web build and the
    Android build. A face outside Google Fonts costs a licence and a change to that pipeline
    on two platforms. Argue for the swap only if the gain is worth that.

Spectral and IBM Plex Mono are working and are not in question.

If you propose a replacement, show one specimen on this board comparing it to Cinzel at the
three sizes the product uses: a 58px headline, the 17px header wordmark at 0.3em, and the
11px divider label at 0.24em. If you would keep Cinzel, say what the drawn wordmark buys
that a typeface change would not, and stop there.

WHAT THE RESEARCH REFUSED, SO IT IS NOT RE-PROPOSED HERE

  The crescent and disc. At 48px it is indistinguishable from the star and crescent, which
  postdates it by over a millennium and has no connection to it.

  The Antikythera mechanism, and gears in general. Conceptually apt for a product that
  computes an ephemeris and wrong in register, a scientific instrument in a surface built
  on shrine and petition. A gear reads as "settings" in any interface, which is the worst
  available outcome for a launcher icon.

  Deity portraits and figures of any kind. Every mark in this set is an instrument or a
  procedure, which keeps the set legible at icon size and keeps the app out of rendering
  gods.

  The ordinary cliches: the ankh and the Eye of Horus, pyramids, columns, laurel, the
  Colosseum, the Capitoline Wolf.

  Refused across the whole product: zodiac clip-art, glowing orbs, starfields, crystal
  balls, purple nebulae.

  - Accent hue is never the only thing separating two marks, both for colourblind readers

  - Notes you write on the canvas follow the product's own copy rule: no em dashes, short
    declarative sentences.
```
