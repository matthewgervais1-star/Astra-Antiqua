# Prompt: the Egypt launcher icon and emblem

Paste into Claude Design. It produces a canvas of artboards, not code.

## Before you paste

This prompt covers **Egypt alone**. The earlier combined brief asked one canvas for all
six launcher icons and organised its requests by section, so the designer put every
culture onto shared comparison boards and no single icon came out as its own exportable
asset. Each culture now gets its own prompt, and each deliverable gets its own artboard.

Four things the fence below carries that a reader of the research would otherwise have to
look up. The mark is closed: the balance of the weighing in its Papyrus of Ani form,
decided by research, not open to exploration. The Android adaptive icon is two layers, so
the foreground and the background are separate artboards and are never flattened together.
The constraints Egypt inherits from the other four cultures are written as absolute
construction rules, because the designer cannot see those cultures from inside this
prompt. The same is done for the fifty small deity glyphs the product already carries,
five of which crowd this mark's shapes.

Eleven artboards come back. Nine are assets. Two are labelled proof sheets and ship
nothing.

Em dash count in this document: zero, per the house voice rules.

```text
You are drawing the launcher icon and the in-app emblem for EGYPT, one culture inside an
ancient-astrology product called Astra Antiqua. This prompt covers Egypt and nothing else.
No other culture's mark may appear anywhere in your output.

THE PRODUCT, IN ONE PARAGRAPH
One sky is computed from a real ephemeris, and five ancient cultures read that same sky
through their own theology. The same planetary placement keeps the same core meaning in
every voice, and each culture's reading is native to its own institutions. The reader picks
a culture and the app's icon changes to match, so each culture's icon is a shipped asset
that has to be recognised alone on a home screen with nothing beside it to explain it. The
product's claim is honesty rather than mysticism: it states plainly that positions are
computed and nothing is invented, and it says so on a method page.

EGYPT'S SURFACE IN THE APP IS THE HALL OF MA'AT, where the day is weighed. The mark has to
be the apparatus that does the weighing, not a metaphor for judgment and not a portrait of
a god.

THE IDENTITY IT ALREADY HAS
The interface is called "The Instrument in the Temple". It is austere: flat culture-tinted
grounds, one muted accent per culture, square corners, no gradients, no shadows, no glass,
no glow. The only ornament is an incised 1px rule. Type is Cinzel for display in caps with
wide tracking, Spectral for prose, IBM Plex Mono for data and microcopy. It reads like a
museum wall label.

EGYPT'S COLOURS, EXACT
  ground  #1A1712
  accent  #B59A55  (gold)
Inks available: bone #E6DECC, prose #D9D1BF, dim #9A917D, faint #6F6B60.
Neutral ground for the monochrome board: #171310.
Rules, if you draw one: rgb(230 222 204 / 0.16).
Use these hex values verbatim. Do not sample, tint, or approximate them.

THE MARK, ALREADY DECIDED
The balance of the weighing, in its Papyrus of Ani form: a feather finial on the upright, a
crossbar carrying two hanging pans, the baboon of Thoth above, and a plumb bob steadied at
the base. It is the device that does the weighing rather than a metaphor for it, and the
most repeated judgment image in the Egyptian corpus across roughly 1500 years. Do not
propose an alternative subject. Your commission is how it is drawn, because it has specific
ways of dying and those failures are the whole job.

HOW THIS MARK FAILS IF DRAWN CARELESSLY
  1. As the scales of justice. This is the named failure mode for this mark and the one
     that costs the most. A bare symmetric beam on a post, two matched pans, nothing else,
     is the courthouse glyph that ships in every icon font. At that point Egypt is a legal
     app and the Hall of Ma'at is gone. Three elements defend against it and none of them
     is optional at 48px: the feather finial, the baboon above it, and a plumb bob that
     hangs visibly off the post's axis. The bob off axis is what says a weighing is in
     progress rather than an abstraction of fairness.
  2. As a compact vessel on a stem. Another culture in this set is also a vertical with
     mass at the top, and its mass is a small shallow bowl. That pair stays open on one
     condition: Egypt's mass is a wide horizontal beam with pans hanging from its ends. If
     the beam narrows, or the apparatus gathers toward the centre, the pair closes.
  3. As a staff with a decorated head. Thin the beam and the pans far enough and an upright
     with a finial is all that reads. Another culture in this set is exactly that, a staff
     carrying a small terminal ornament, so this failure hands Egypt's icon to a different
     surface in the same product.
  4. As two plumes. This product also carries a small Egyptian glyph built from two tall
     parallel feathers rising from a short base. If the balance's finial doubles, the icon
     collides with it. One feather blade, alone, always.
  5. As raised arms cradling a disc. This product also carries a small Egyptian glyph of
     two arms opening upward around a small circle. Pans drawn as two shapes sitting on top
     of the beam produce that shape exactly. Everything on this mark that hangs, hangs below
     the beam.
  6. As a disc, or a crescent holding a disc. This product also carries small Egyptian
     glyphs for both. Pans drawn as filled circles or as deep cupping crescents walk into
     them. The pans are shallow open trays.
  7. As mud, which is what the baboon becomes if it is drawn as an animal portrait and then
     scaled down. It has to be built as a small silhouette from the start and it has to
     survive, because it is one of the three things holding failure 1 shut.

CONSTRUCTION RULES, ABSOLUTE
These are the rules the drawing has to obey. They are stated as rules rather than as
comparisons because you cannot see the other four cultures, or the fifty small deity
glyphs, from here.

  The beam is the widest element on the board and the mark's centre of gravity. One
  straight horizontal crossbar. Its span is never less than 70 percent of the mark's total
  width. Nothing about this mark may gather into a compact mass at the top.

  Two pans hang BELOW the beam, one from each end, on visible cords. Nothing on this mark
  sits above the beam except the post, the finial and the baboon. A pan is a shallow open
  tray, an arc with a flat or near-flat floor, wider than it is deep. A pan is never a
  filled circle, never a closed ring, never a deep crescent cup. The two pans hang at
  slightly different heights, so the apparatus reads as loaded rather than as a diagram of
  equality.

  The post is a single straight vertical upright, from the base to the middle of the beam.
  Straight at every size. It never curves, and it never ends in a spiral or a ball. This
  set gives the curved staff with a terminal spiral to one culture only and Egypt is not
  it.

  One feather finial at the top of the post: a single upright blade, rounded at the top,
  with a visible central shaft, leaning slightly off vertical the way a real ostrich
  feather does. One blade. Never a matched pair, never a fan, never a splayed crest.

  The baboon of Thoth sits above the finial, seated in profile, facing whichever way you
  choose, held to that choice on every board. Build it as a silhouette: a hunched back
  line, a forward muzzle notch, a squared haunch, forelimbs down. It stays small against
  the beam, roughly one fifth of the beam's span, so it reads as an attribute perched on
  the instrument rather than as a creature holding it. At 48px it reduces to a solid mass
  with the muzzle notch and the back step still cut into it. It is never dropped, and it
  never grows into a head-and-shoulders portrait.

  The plumb bob hangs from a short bracket on one side of the post, near the base, so its
  cord runs parallel to the post and visibly beside it. The cord and the post are never
  collinear. The bob is a small solid drop. This offset is load-bearing and survives every
  reduction, including 48px.

  The base is a plain foot: a short horizontal ground line, or a low stepped plinth of two
  strokes at most. It is never a rectilinear seat with a back seen in profile, which is
  another Egyptian glyph in this product.

  No hieroglyphic inscription, no cartouche, no name band, no lettering of any kind. This
  set gives letterforms to one culture only and Egypt is not it.

  No stars, no rays, no dots scattered along an arc. Another Egyptian glyph in this product
  is a shallow arc studded with small stars, and this mark stays clear of it.

  One stroke weight throughout, an incised line of even width. Egyptian attributes reach us
  through painted papyrus and incised relief, so the hand is a drawn line: even width,
  closed forms, no bevels, no tapering flourishes. At 512 the stroke reads about 20 to 24
  units. At 48 it never falls below 3px after every scaling step.

  Optical vertical. The mark stands upright, centred on its own optical weight, with the
  beam and the hanging pans counted. A geometric centre will look bottom-heavy; correct by
  eye.

COLOUR RULE
Draw the mark in accent #B59A55 on ground #1A1712. A second ink is permitted for the baboon
only, from bone #E6DECC, and only if the mark's silhouette is unchanged when the whole
thing collapses to one flat ink. Hue may never be the only thing carrying the mark, both
for colourblind readers and because monochrome contexts ship.

THE EXPORT CONTRACT, WHICH IS THE POINT OF THIS PROMPT
Every deliverable asset gets its own artboard. One mark per board. Name each board with the
exact name given below, spelled as written. Composite boards are allowed only on the two
boards labelled proof, and a proof sheet is never an asset. Boards 1 through 9 each contain
a single instance of a single mark and nothing else: no comparison strip, no family row, no
size ladder, no annotation inside the live area.

ARTBOARDS TO PRODUCE

  1. icon-egypt-512
     512x512. The launcher mark on ground #1A1712, mark in accent #B59A55. Mark occupies
     roughly 60 to 70 percent of the canvas width, optically centred. This is the master
     asset.

  2. icon-egypt-fg
     108x108. The SAME mark as the Android adaptive-icon FOREGROUND LAYER ONLY.
     Transparent background, no ground fill of any kind. Every part that carries meaning
     sits inside the middle 72x72; the outer 18 units on each side are masked away by the
     launcher and may hold nothing load-bearing. Keep the beam's two ends, the pans and the
     baboon inside the safe zone with room to spare, since some launchers crop harder than
     the spec and the beam is the widest thing here.

  3. icon-egypt-bg
     108x108. The adaptive-icon BACKGROUND LAYER: a flat fill of #1A1712, edge to edge.
     Nothing else on this board. No mark, no rule, no texture, no vignette.

  4. icon-egypt-mask-circle
     The composed icon, foreground over background, under a circular launcher mask. Present
     at 512x512 with the mask boundary at the artboard edge and nothing outside it. This
     board proves the safe zone held, which for Egypt means the beam's ends survived the
     circle.

  5. icon-egypt-mask-squircle
     The same composed icon under a squircle mask, presented the same way.

  6. icon-egypt-48
     48x48. The mark at the size that ships, drawn for that size rather than
     scaled down from 512. Thin the detail until the wide beam, the two hanging pans, the
     single feather, the baboon silhouette and the off-axis plumb bob all still read. Those
     five are the mark; anything else may go. One instance only; the size ladder belongs on
     board 11.

  7. icon-egypt-mono
     512x512. The mark in bone #E6DECC alone on neutral ground #171310, for notification
     icons and favicons where accent hue is gone. Single flat ink, no second value, no
     grey, which means the baboon holds on silhouette alone here. If the mark needs its
     accent to hold together, fix the mark, not this board.

  8. emblem-egypt-header
     640x160. THE TIER 2 EMBLEM: the wedjat, the Eye of Horus. This is a different mark
     from the launcher icon and appears only here and on board 9. Draw it in profile: an
     almond eye with a pupil, a brow line above and clear of the lid, a straight marking
     dropping below the front of the eye, and a curled spiral sweeping back and down from
     the rear corner. Both markings are drawn at this scale. No triangle around it, no
     rays, no enclosing frame, no lettering. Fix its facing direction here and keep the
     same direction on board 9. Place it at header scale, roughly 32 units tall, beside its
     label set in Cinzel caps with wide tracking: EGYPT, with THE HALL OF MA'AT beneath in
     IBM Plex Mono, smaller, in dim #9A917D. Ground #1A1712, wedjat in accent #B59A55,
     label in bone #E6DECC. The label is why a famous mark is safe here when it would not
     be safe alone on a home screen. The wedjat is never a launcher icon and never appears
     on boards 1 through 7, and the balance never appears on this board or the next.
     Two things to hold. The product already carries a much smaller wedjat, roughly 16 to
     24px, as one of its planetary glyphs, drawn with the brow, the eye and one marking
     only. This emblem is the same subject at header scale with both markings present, so
     draw it with enough detail that the two are never mistaken for the same asset. And the
     ankh, which the research keeps for surfaces about life rather than judgment, is out of
     scope here. Do not substitute it.

  9. emblem-egypt-share
     800x500. The same wedjat at share-card scale, roughly 128 units tall, on ground
     #1A1712, with the label lower in the field and an incised 1px rule at
     rgb(230 222 204 / 0.16) if the composition needs one. More detail is allowed here: the
     lid's inner line, the cosmetic band along the brow. Same subject, same construction,
     same facing, nothing new introduced.

 10. proof-egypt-failure
     A PROOF SHEET, not an asset. 1600x800. The correct mark drawn once at the left, then
     seven failure specimens beside it, each one a wrongly drawn Egyptian balance, each
     labelled with the failure name and a one-line note in IBM Plex Mono saying what in the
     correct construction defends against it. Do not draw any other culture's mark, or any
     of the product's small deity glyphs, on this board as a reference specimen; the
     failures are drawn as bad balances, not as the marks they drift toward.
       SCALES OF JUSTICE. Bare symmetric beam, two matched pans, no feather, no baboon, a
         centred plumb line. Defence: the feather finial, the baboon above it, the off-axis
         bob, and pans hanging at unequal heights.
       COMPACT VESSEL. The mass gathered into a small bowl on a stem. Defence: the beam
         holds at least 70 percent of the total width and the pans hang from its ends.
       STAFF WITH A HEAD. Beam and pans thinned to nothing beside an upright with a finial.
         Defence: the beam is the widest element on the board and the pans never leave it.
       TWIN PLUMES. Two feathers on the finial. Defence: one blade, alone.
       RAISED ARMS. Pans drawn as two shapes opening upward off the beam. Defence:
         everything that hangs, hangs below the beam on visible cords.
       FILLED DISCS. Pans drawn as solid circles, or as deep crescent cups. Defence:
         shallow open trays with a flat floor, wider than they are deep.
       BABOON AS MUD. The baboon drawn as a portrait, then scaled until it is a blot.
         Defence: it is built as a silhouette at 48px first, at one fifth of the beam's
         span, with the muzzle notch and the back step cut in.

 11. proof-egypt-scale
     A PROOF SHEET, not an asset. 1200x400. The mark at 512, 108, 48 and 24 in one row on
     ground #1A1712, each at true relative size, each labelled with its pixel size beneath
     in IBM Plex Mono. The point of this board is to make the failure point visible, so do
     not thicken the small sizes to flatter them. The baboon and the plumb bob are the two
     likeliest casualties; show what they do at each step rather than hiding it. If the
     mark stops holding at 24, show that it stops and say in a note which element goes
     first.

WHAT THE RESEARCH REFUSED, SO YOU DO NOT RE-PROPOSE IT
  The ankh and the Eye of Horus as the launcher icon. Both are tier 2 marks that need a
  label beside them, which is why board 8 exists and boards 1 through 7 do not carry them.
  Pyramids, obelisks, columns, the sphinx, the ordinary stock-photo furniture of Egypt.
  The crescent and disc. At 48px it is indistinguishable from the star and crescent, which
  postdates it by over a millennium and has no connection to it.
  The Antikythera mechanism. Conceptually apt for an app that computes an ephemeris and the
  wrong register entirely, a scientific instrument in a surface built on shrine and
  petition. A gear reads as "settings" in any interface.
  Zodiac clip-art, glowing orbs, starfields, crystal balls, purple nebulae. This product's
  differentiator is that it is not that.
  Deity portraits. Every launcher mark in this set is an instrument or a procedure, which
  keeps the set legible at icon size and keeps the app out of rendering gods. Anubis
  adjusting the plummet, Thoth in ibis-headed human form and Ma'at herself all stay off
  this canvas. The baboon on the balance is an attribute perched on the instrument, and it
  stays that. The wedjat on boards 8 and 9 is an attribute beside a label, and it stays
  there.

CONSTRAINTS
  Square corners everywhere in the interface. The icon is exempt, since platform masks
  round it anyway; design for the mask rather than fighting it.
  No gradients, no drop shadows, no bevels, no glow, on any board.
  Everything survives being flat. This identity has no depth to hide behind.
  Hue is never the only thing separating one mark from another.

IF YOU THINK A DECISION HERE IS WRONG, write a note on the relevant proof sheet saying so.
Do not quietly draw something else.
```
